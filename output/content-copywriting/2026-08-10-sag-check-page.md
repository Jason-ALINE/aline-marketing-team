Every alignment bracket sags a little under its own weight — the longer the span between indicator and bracket, the more it sags. Don't account for it, and that sag shows up in your readings as vertical misalignment that isn't really there. You'll end up "correcting" a problem that doesn't exist. Run this check before you take your final readings on [Vertical Move](/vertical-move).

Haven't mounted your brackets and measured A, B, and C yet? Start on the [Introduction](/introduction) page first — you need the same bracket span for this check to mean anything.

## BEFORE YOU START

- Your bracket set and indicators, mounted at the same span (the A distance) you measured on the Introduction page
- A sturdy piece of pipe or rod to clamp the brackets to for the test

## Procedure

1. Clamp the brackets onto the pipe at the same distance apart they'll be when mounted on the actual equipment.
2. Zero both indicators at the top (12 o'clock).
3. Rotate both indicators to the bottom (6 o'clock) and read them.
4. The difference between the top and bottom reading is the sag.

## SAG CHECK FORMULA

```
Sag = Top reading − Bottom reading
Sag is always a negative value.
```

Because sag is always negative, when you get to the [Vertical Move](/vertical-move) page, start your dial indicator with a plus (+) reading to offset it — that's what keeps the bracket's own sag from reading as false misalignment.

## Why this matters before final bolt-up

Sag isn't a defect — it's physics, and every bracket has some. The point of this check isn't to eliminate it, it's to know the number so you can subtract it out before you trust a vertical reading. Skipping this step is one of the more common reasons a "good" alignment on paper doesn't hold up once the machine's running.
