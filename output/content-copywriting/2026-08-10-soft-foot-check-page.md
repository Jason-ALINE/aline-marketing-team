Soft foot means one of the machine's feet isn't sitting flat on the baseplate — the foot or the base has warped, or there's something trapped underneath it. Bolt a soft foot down and you drag the shaft centerline out of position the moment you torque it. No amount of careful work on the Horizontal Move or Vertical Move pages will hold if a soft foot is fighting you underneath.

Check for soft foot before you trust any alignment reading, and before final bolt-up.

## BEFORE YOU START

- Dial indicator with a magnetic base
- Shim stock, various thicknesses
- Wrench sized for the base bolts
- Torque wrench, for final retightening to spec

## Procedure

1. Set the dial indicator to the 12 o'clock position against the foot you're checking, depress it, and zero it.
2. Loosen that foot's base bolt.
3. Watch the indicator. If it moves away from zero, that foot is soft — and the amount it moves is how much shim you need under that foot to correct it.
4. Slide in shims to that thickness.
5. Retighten the bolt to spec and confirm the indicator needle doesn't move. If it holds at zero, the soft foot is corrected.
6. Repeat for each remaining foot.

## Why this matters before you align

A soft foot changes shape every time that bolt is torqued or loosened — which means it changes your indicator readings on every page after this one. Confirming it's corrected here is what makes the Horizontal Move and Vertical Move numbers trustworthy later.
