### 1.3 Turnaround-timing hook — use as a question, not a claim
Research flags turnaround-season timing as a general industry pattern, not a confirmed Oklahoma refinery schedule (unverified list, item 6). **Do not state a specific outage window as fact.** The sequence above avoids naming a season. If a call happens, the right move is to *ask* Matrix Service when their next Oklahoma-area outage window is, not to assume spring/fall turnaround timing and lead with it. If a contact confirms a real window, that's the trigger to loop in Paid Ads / Content for a timed push — not before.

### 1.4 Buy-vs-hire objection — full framing (for use beyond this sequence too)
**Note: this is a different objection from the replace-vs-add-on framing in Touch 1/2 above — don't conflate them.** Replace-vs-add-on is about whether ALINE competes with or complements a laser tool the prospect already owns (Section 1.2's note). Buy-vs-hire is about whether the prospect equips their own crew at all, or pays a third party to do the alignment work entirely. A prospect could raise either, both, or neither — the add-on framing doesn't resolve the hire-a-service question, and vice versa.

This is a different competitive conversation than tool-vs-tool (laser vs. reverse-indicator). The competitor here is a service model (e.g., Alignment Services USA — HQ and Oklahoma track record unconfirmed per research), not another tool vendor. Talking points to use consistently across any contract-firm-PM outreach:
- **Schedule risk**: hired alignment services depend on a third party's availability; owning the tool removes that dependency during compressed turnaround windows.
- **Repeatability**: a firm running multiple turnarounds a year gets the same method and standard (reverse-indicator, API 686/ANSI S2.75) on every job regardless of which crew or which service vendor shows up.
- **Do not invent a cost comparison.** We don't have verified pricing data on alignment-service day rates vs. tool ownership cost — no dollar-figure claim should go in outreach copy until that's actually sourced. If Jason wants this argument to include real numbers, that's a research/costing task, not something to fabricate in a cold email.
