## 1. Bottom line for outreach (read this section first)

- Oklahoma has a real, concentrated industrial base for shaft alignment tools: refining (3-4 plants), gas processing (dozens of ONEOK plants + others), power generation (OG&E + AEP/PSO stations), a very large aviation MRO base, and a defense depot doing turbine engine overhaul. This is not a thin market — it's a legitimate expansion target on industrial density alone.
- **Best-fit outreach wedge: contract maintenance/turnaround firms, not just plants directly.** Matrix Service Company — a major North American industrial maintenance/turnaround/EPC contractor — is headquartered in Tulsa. That's a contract-firm project manager audience sitting in-state, which matches ALINE's actual buyer persona directly (see company-brief.md: contract-firm PMs are a named buyer, not an afterthought).
- Refinery/gas-plant turnaround season is the highest-probability buying window — turnarounds are when contract millwrights and PMs are actively re-equipping or renting/buying alignment tools on short cycles. This is a timing hook Outreach can use, not just a target-list hook.
- Motion Industries and Grainger both have confirmed physical Oklahoma branches already. If ALINE wants Oklahoma sales without building direct local sales infrastructure, routing through Motion's existing OKC/Broken Arrow branches (and any other OK branches not yet confirmed) is a channel play worth Outreach evaluating now — the infrastructure already exists, ALINE doesn't need to build it. **(2026-08-03: additional branches likely exist beyond OKC/Broken Arrow — see Section 9.1.)**
- No evidence found of a reverse-indicator-method tool competitor with a dedicated Oklahoma sales presence. The players who do show up locally are laser-alignment vendors and alignment *service* providers — different purchase decision (buy-a-tool vs. hire-a-service), and worth treating as a different competitive category, not a head-to-head substitute. See Section 4.

### Gas processing / midstream
- ONEOK alone operates dozens of gas processing plants in Oklahoma (named examples: Woodward, Maysville, Antelope Hills, Custer) plus storage fields at Haskell, Osage, Edmond, and Depew. ONEOK's pipeline system connects to 35 processing plants and 130 producing fields statewide (per ONEOK's own materials). Concentration is heaviest in the gas-producing western half of the state.
- Oklahoma is the 3rd-largest natural gas producer and 5th-largest crude oil producer in the US (2023 figure, $57B economic contribution) — scale context, not a facility count.

### Power generation
- OG&E: 10 generating stations, ~6,300 MW aggregate, all located in Oklahoma.
- AEP/PSO: ~4,400 MW of generating capacity; includes Riverside (Jenks) and Southwestern (Washita) stations, plus the newly acquired Green Country plant (795 MW, Jenks).

### Aviation MRO / defense (high rotating-equipment-maintenance density, non-obvious segment)
- American Airlines Tech Ops — Tulsa: world's largest airline-owned maintenance base, ~5,000 employees including 3,200+ licensed maintenance technicians. This is a huge concentration of hands-on mechanical maintenance labor, though the direct fit for *shaft alignment* tools (vs. airframe/avionics work) is unverified — flagging as a segment worth a direct verification call, not assuming fit. **(2026-08-03: engine-overhaul-specific fit substantially clarified — see Section 9.3.)**
- Tinker AFB / Oklahoma City Air Logistics Complex: largest single-site employer in Oklahoma (26,000+ workers), the nation's largest DoD aircraft/jet engine MRO depot, handles turbine engine overhaul for multiple engine families. Turbine engine overhaul is a much more direct shaft-alignment-relevant activity than airframe MRO — worth flagging as a lead, though government/DoD procurement is a very different sales motion than industrial plant procurement and would need its own path (likely not a fit for Outreach's standard playbook). **(2026-08-03: still unconfirmed as a procurement signal — see Section 9.3.)**

### General manufacturing
- Michelin (Ardmore) — tire manufacturing, 2,500+ employees at that site.
- Amcor (Pauls Valley) — large packaging manufacturer.
- IC Bus of Oklahoma (Tulsa) — bus manufacturing.
- MidAmerica Industrial Park (Pryor) — 1,500-acre industrial park with Google, Siemens, CBC Global Ammunition tenants.
- McAlester Army Ammunition Plant — active DoD ammunition production facility.

**Read on this:** the refining, gas processing, and power generation segments are the strongest fit for shaft alignment tools (large rotating equipment — pumps, compressors, turbines). General manufacturing and aviation MRO are plausible but unverified as direct fits and shouldn't be treated as equal-priority targets without more digging.

This is carried forward from general industrial-maintenance-market knowledge, **not verified against an actual Oklahoma buyer conversation** — flag as assumption, not fact, until Outreach gets it confirmed in a real conversation.
